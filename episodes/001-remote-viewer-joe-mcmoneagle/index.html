  • Joe McMoneagle served as a remote viewer for U.S. Army intelligence and was the first recruit in the program later known as Stargate. He explains that it began as Gondola Wish, a two-year threat assessment that continued for 23 years because the results proved unexpectedly useful.
  • He argues that time and space are not fixed realities but a matrix created by our actions and decisions. Drawing on a near-death experience in which he was reportedly without a heartbeat or breath for 38 minutes, he describes encountering an instantaneous review of one's life and its effects on others.
  • The conversation explores how remote viewing works, why consent matters, classified work such as monitoring the Tehran hostages, research at the Monroe Institute, and the differences among remote viewing, out-of-body experiences, lucid dreaming, and near-death experiences.
  • Later, the discussion turns to nonhuman intelligence, nuclear weapons, and risks to human civilization. McMoneagle's closing advice is to give up revenge and conflict, act with integrity, and take care of one another.
